Tote pools

Everyone buys in, the pool splits between backers of the winner, the house takes nothing. Implied odds move as money lands; the model price beside each is what the simulation says is fair. Where implied is longer than fair, that outcome is underbacked.

Form book

Built from 411 individual rounds pulled from Squabbit across every player's whole history — not just the trips. Only 56 of them are competition rounds; the other 355 are their own golf at home. Pairs-format rounds are excluded, because identical scores across a pairing say nothing about one player, and that removes 50 more.

Players

Form is a recency-weighted scoring differential, lower is better. Raw is form minus handicap straight from the data; Gap is that after thin samples are discounted toward the field median — three rounds should not carry the same weight as sixty-eight. Spread is day-to-day volatility.

Model prices

Monte Carlo of the full Ryder Cup: foursomes, fourball, scramble and singles, hole by hole, with pairings redrawn every simulation so the prices average over who ends up with whom.

How the pools work

  • Every market is a tote pool. Stakes in, pool split between backers of the winning outcome in proportion to what they staked. Nothing is deducted.
  • The organiser takes no commission and holds no position against the field. A sweepstake, not a book.
  • Stakes are in whole pounds, minimum £1. Back as many outcomes as you like.
  • All stakes collected before the first tee shot. Nobody plays on credit.
  • Ante-post runs from now until the handicaps are declared on the first night. You are betting against a model built on last season's numbers and some assumptions, so stakes struck in this window count for a larger share of the pool when it divides. Betting blind should pay something.
  • Ante-post prices are deliberately flatter than the ones you will see later. The model re-draws every handicap and every form figure on each simulation to reflect that none of it is settled, so the favourite is longer and the outsider shorter than the eventual price. That is not a margin — it is the model declining to sound certain when it isn't.
  • Open runs from declaration until the first tee shot on Thursday. Handicaps are known and the prices are real, so stakes take an ordinary share. Later means safer and cheaper to judge, but a thinner slice.
  • Closed. There is no in-play betting. Whole-trip markets — MVP, Team Result, Top Scorers, Perfect Record, The Blank — shut at the first tee shot on Thursday, because after that people are backing things they have watched. A round's own markets shut when that round tees off, so the Sunday singles book stays open until Sunday morning while Thursday's shut on Thursday. All of it happens automatically off the tee times.
  • Both phases feed the same pool on each market. The phase changes your share of it, not what you are betting on, and none of it goes to the organiser.
  • Prices are informational. Your return depends on the pool, not the model.

Scoring

  • 25 points available: five foursomes, five fourball, five scramble, ten singles. A win is one point, a half is a half. 13 to win, 12.5 to retain.
  • MVP is most points contributed across the four sessions. Maximum four.
  • Ties on points are settled by head-to-head singles result, then by a coin toss in the bar.

Settlement

  • Dead heat. Pool splits equally between tied outcomes, then between backers within each.
  • Non-runner. Anyone who does not tee it up at all is refunded in full on every market naming them. Stakes on other outcomes stand.
  • Withdrawal mid-trip. Points already banked stand; missed sessions score zero. Backers are not refunded — you backed a man who might get injured.
  • Abandoned session. That session's matches are halved and its markets settle on the half.
  • Scoring record. Squabbit is the record. If Squabbit and a paper card disagree, Squabbit wins.
  • Handicaps are locked at the airport and not revisited, whatever happens on Thursday.
  • Novelty markets are settled by the organiser on the evidence available. Video beats testimony.
  • Disputes go to the organiser. Any dispute involving the organiser goes to the two captains; if they cannot agree the pool is voided and refunded.

Private betting between friends is lawful in the UK and winnings are untaxed. What would change that is the organiser taking a cut or building a margin — that is bookmaking and needs a licence. Hence no rake, ever.

Roster

Change a handicap and every price moves. Form is expected scoring differential, pre-filled from history — nudge it if someone has clearly changed. Players marked form assumed have a handicap but no logged rounds, so their scoring is the field-median assumption rather than anything measured.

Payment

Starling Settle Up is the tool: it produces a secure link, Starling users pay in the app and everyone else pays by debit card on a web page, so nobody needs to be a customer. Create it once, paste it here, and it shows as a Pay button on the Pools tab. Money lands in your main account — sweep it into a Chip & Sip Space yourself, because Spaces cannot be paid into from outside.

Ledger

Straight from the database, so it counts every bet anyone has struck on any phone. Tap the button to record someone as settled up.

Every bet

Void anything struck in error. Voided bets leave the pools and the ledger immediately but stay in the database, so there is always a record of what happened.

Courses and format

Handicap declaration

Bets taken before the first night are ante-post: struck while the handicaps are still guesses. Flip this on once they are declared in the bar, then correct the handicaps above and re-run — every price moves and the board reprices itself.

Blind uncertainty is how much the model widens its prices while handicaps are undeclared. Every simulation draws each player a slightly different handicap and form — about a shot either way for the sixteen with a record, considerably more for the four without. Favourites drift, longshots come in, and nothing pretends to a precision it has not got. Tick Handicaps declared and it switches off, so the board sharpens the moment you know something. Set it to 0 to disable.

The book shuts by itself at the first tee shot on Thursday, so you do not have to remember. Close it early if you want. The override is for fixing a stake you keyed into the wrong column — leave it off the rest of the time so you cannot do it by accident.

A tote has no concept of taking a price: everyone in a pool gets the same terms whenever they bet, so without this an ante-post bet is simply a worse bet — same return, less information. The multiplier fixes that by giving early money a larger share of the same pool. At 1.25 a pound staked blind counts as £1.25 when the pool is divided. It moves money between backers rather than to the organiser, so there is still no rake. Set it to 1 to switch the whole idea off.
